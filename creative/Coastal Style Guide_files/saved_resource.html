Mark

"Pulse Beacon" — a pointed-peak shield with the lighthouse cut out as negative space: lantern, beams, and diagonal-striped tower all knocked out of the sky-blue fill, with a coral heart glowing where the lantern sits. This is the production geometry, round 22 — pulled directly from the logo board.

Coastal Healthcare Advocates

Shield: pointed-peak container, locked since round 11. Lighthouse, beams, and tower stripe are cut from the fill as true negative space — not drawn on top.

Tower: diagonal stripe rebuilt flush with the collar in round 22, no visible seam gap. Heart: positive element glowing in the lantern, geometry unchanged since round 5, now filled coral so it reads as the mark's one warm note against the cool shield. Stripe token resolves per theme — navy in light mode, fixed sky in dark — so it always reads as a second tone against the shield.

Wordmark: flat ink fill, no gradient sweep, no spark dot (both dropped round 21–22). Full production file, colourway variants, and favicon set live on the logo board.

Type scale

Every size on this page comes off one modular scale — a base size multiplied by a single consistent ratio — rather than being picked by eye. That is what keeps a long page feeling composed instead of assembled.

1.25
Major third · base 17 px

The standard ratio for modern web work — bolder than the 1.2 minor third often used for premium brands, short of the drama of 1.5. It earns its place here for a specific reason: 1.25 off a 17 px base reproduces the sizes this brand already uses — the 11 px eyebrow, the 17 px body, and the 81 px display that Cormorant was specced to at its largest. The scale was reverse-engineered from the identity rather than imposed on it.

Measure, not just size

Running text stops at 65 characters. A 17 px body line that runs the full width of a desktop page is unreadable no matter how correct its size is — the scale governs height, the measure governs width.

The golden ratio is for layout, not type

Use 1.618 where the page splits: a 1000 px canvas divides into a 618 px main column and a 382 px sidebar. Keep it out of the type scale, where 1.25 already does the work.

Alternative palettes — proposed

Three palettes put forward as alternatives to Blue Job, each shown carrying the mark and the horizontal lockup on a light and a dark ground from its own set. Nothing here is adopted — Blue Job in §01 remains the identity. These exist to be judged against it.

One note that applies to all three: none of the supplied sets includes a light paper value, so each proposal below uses a ground derived from its own palette. That value would need to be agreed before any of these could be specified.

Cool sage over deep teal, with taupe as the one warm-leaning value. It reads calm and clinical-adjacent without the chill of a pure blue, and the pine near-black is a richer dark ground than midnight.

What it costs: taupe is a warm neutral, not a warm accent. The heart stops being the one bright point in the mark and sinks into the shield's value range — exactly the focal point rounds 17–23 were spent protecting. It also moves the lighthouse away from water-blue, which is most of what makes the mark read as coastal.

The nearest relative to Blue Job — same blue family, lower saturation, with sand as a warm paper option. This is what the current identity would look like if it were dialled down and aged twenty years: quieter, more traditional, more established.

Heart in Ink #061221. This solves the contrast problem the sand heart had — ink against the pale haze ground is the strongest value jump available in the set, and the heart reads cleanly at small sizes again. On the dark ground ink is the ground, so the mapping inverts and the heart takes Haze #DDE8ED; the stripe stays Deep in both modes.

What it costs: the heart becomes a dark focal point rather than a warm one. That is a legitimate direction — it is what the One colour treatment already does — but it changes what the mark means: instead of a warm light held inside a cool shield, you get a tonal mark where the heart reads as weight rather than heat. Sand then has no job left in the palette except as paper.

The only one of the three that keeps the mark's structure intact. Terracotta #BD674A does precisely the job Coral does now — the heart stays the single warm point in a cool mark — and the tide/deep-tide pair gives the shield and its stripe two distinct values in both modes without needing the light/dark token split. Forest green is a dark ground that isn't another navy.

What it costs: terracotta is muted where coral is bright, so the heart carries less urgency at favicon size; and the forest ground pulls the brand toward land where the lighthouse wants water. Both are liveable. If the palette is genuinely in play, this is the one to develop.

↳

The test any replacement has to pass. The mark is built around one warm point inside a cool shield — that is the whole reason the heart reads before anything else does, at every size down to a favicon. Sea Glass fails it: taupe is a neutral, and substituting one turns the heart into a hole. Harbor Light with an ink heart passes on contrast but not on warmth — the heart is legible, but the mark becomes tonal rather than warm-point-in-cool-shield. Tidewater is the only one that passes outright, because terracotta is a real accent doing Coral's job.

If the intent is a warmer or more muted brand rather than a different one, the cheaper move is to keep Blue Job's structure and shift only its two neutrals — Paper and Midnight — toward the sand and pine tones above, leaving Sky and Coral to do the work they already do.
