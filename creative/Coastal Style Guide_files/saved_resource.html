02

Mark

"Advocate Beacon" — a pointed-peak shield with the lighthouse cut out as negative space: lantern, beams, and the spiralling tower stripes all knocked out of the sky-blue fill, with a coral heart glowing where the lantern sits. The name carries the mark’s argument: a beacon is what stands watch and speaks up for the people navigating past it. This is the production geometry.

Coastal Healthcare Advocates

Shield: pointed-peak container. Lighthouse, beams, and tower stripe are cut from the fill as true negative space — not drawn on top. Where the beams and finial meet the shield edge, the resulting corners are softened on the composed silhouette rather than on any source path, so the mark holds together at small sizes without the joins going sharp.

Tower: the stripes are helices on the tower surface rather than flat diagonals — a point at angle t projects to x = c(y) + w(y)·sin t, so each band flattens as it reaches the silhouette and reads as wrapping around the tower. One revolution over the tower height. They meet the collar flush, with no seam gap. Heart: positive element glowing in the lantern, filled coral so it reads as the mark's one warm note against the cool shield. Stripe: every colourway takes the shield's own tone, so the bands read as shield showing through a cut-out tower rather than as a second colour drawn on it. Tower and stripes are one path, the stripes punched out of it, so the mark is a single stencil at any size.

Wordmark: flat ink fill, no gradient sweep, no spark dot. Full production file, colourway variants, and the favicon set ship together as a single asset package.

Type scale

Every size on this page comes off one modular scale — a base size multiplied by a single consistent ratio — rather than being picked by eye. That is what keeps a long page feeling composed instead of assembled.

1.25
Major third · base 17 px

The standard ratio for modern web work — bolder than the 1.2 minor third often used for premium brands, short of the drama of 1.5. It earns its place here for a specific reason: 1.25 off a 17 px base reproduces the sizes this brand already uses — the 11 px eyebrow, the 17 px body, and the 65 px H1 that tops the scale. The scale was reverse-engineered from the identity rather than imposed on it.

Nine columns

Website layout runs on a 9-column grid. Nine divides cleanly into thirds, which is what most of the site actually needs — three service tiles, a two-thirds article beside a one-third rail — and it keeps the page from drifting into the twelve-column default where every span is available and nothing is decided.

Branded products

The identity carried onto physical goods. Each piece names the colourway it uses, so a reorder can be specified from this page alone.

Stationery — letterhead, card, USB
Horizontal lockup across the set, with the mark alone on the USB. The card carries the full contact block plus a QR to the site. Letterhead and USB take the mark in Navy; the card takes it in Sky. Shown on a warm cream stock that predates Paper #F5FBFF — a reprint on the current value will read noticeably cooler.
stock #F5FBFF card shield #4FACFE letterhead + USB #0F1E4A heart #FE1E1A horizontal lockup
Caps — navy and white twill
Mark only, no wordmark, identical on both grounds. Embroidery is the one process that cannot hold the knocked-out stripe as shield-through-tower, so the bands are stitched in white and the tower reads as a light column — the only sanctioned departure from the stripe rule. Keep the mark at 55 mm or wider so the bands stay separated in thread.
shield #4FACFE stripe stitched white heart #FE1E1A min width 55 mm
Apparel — polo and tote
Reversed colourway on a Navy ground: one ink, white, with the shield fill dropped out entirely so the garment shows through the tower and stripes. Polo takes the stacked lockup at chest scale; the tote splits it — mark above, wordmark below — at a size where the helix stays fully legible.
one colour white ground #0F1E4A stacked lockup reversed
Tumbler — laser-etched steel
Etch on a powder-coated body: the mark is ablated to bare steel, so the shield reads as brushed metal and the stripes as remaining coat. The heart is the single colour fill, in Cyan rather than Coral — coral infill on black goes muddy under warm light, cyan holds. Mark only; no wordmark at this diameter.
