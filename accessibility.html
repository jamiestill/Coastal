Our commitment

Coastal Healthcare Advocates is committed to making coastalhealthcareadvocates.org usable for as many people as possible, including people who rely on assistive technology. We treat digital accessibility as part of doing this work well: many of the people who come to us are already dealing with illness, disability, or the stress of a denied claim, and the site should not add to that.

Standard we aim for

We design and build this site to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1, Level AA, and we use those guidelines together with the accessibility expectations of the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act as our reference points.

What that means on this site

  • A single, clearly structured page with proper headings and landmark regions.
  • Full keyboard operation, including the navigation, the FAQs, and the contact form.
  • A visible focus outline on every interactive element.
  • Text and controls that meet AA colour-contrast ratios, and body text that stays readable when the page is zoomed to 200% or more.
  • Respect for the "reduce motion" setting in your operating system.
  • Labels, hints, and error messages on every form field.

Known limitations

We are not aware of significant accessibility barriers on this site at this time. If a formal audit identifies issues, they will be listed here along with the plan and timeline for fixing them. PDF documents linked from this site (for example a profile or pricing sheet) may not yet be fully tagged for screen readers; contact us and we will provide the information in an accessible format.

Feedback and getting help

If you run into an accessibility barrier on this site, or you need information from it in a different format — large print, plain text, or read aloud over the phone — please tell us. We aim to respond within two business days.

If we don't resolve it

If you contact us about an accessibility problem and you are not satisfied with our response, you can raise a complaint with the U.S. Department of Justice, Civil Rights Division, which enforces the Americans with Disabilities Act, at ada.gov. This does not affect any other legal rights you may have.
