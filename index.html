What is a healthcare advocate?

A healthcare advocate works only for you, not for the hospital or the insurance company.

  • Your guide through the healthcare maze

    We help you with bills, benefits, and denied claims.

  • Someone in your corner when it matters

    Independent advocacy for the insurance and billing decisions that can't wait.

  • Clarity when healthcare gets confusing

    Someone who has worked inside hospital billing and claims, now working on your behalf.

  • Healthcare has a lot of fine print. We read it.

    We handle insurance appeals, billing disputes, and medical debt.

You keep every decision. We handle the paperwork, phone calls, and follow-up.

Why a person, not a free artificial intelligence appeal tool?

  • A person can call your insurer and negotiate for you. An AI tool can only draft a letter.
  • Insurers use AI too, and their systems can flag appeal letters that were written by AI.
  • Cases with several providers or a lot of money at stake need judgment a template can't give.
  • We manage the case over weeks, through every reply and appeal level.
  • We're local and accountable, and Lindsey spent 18 years working inside hospital billing.
  • We follow HIPAA privacy standards. Your medical records stay protected, not uploaded to an app.

Lindsey Hewitt

Healthcare Advocate

Lindsey Hewitt brings 18 years of experience in medical billing, insurance claims, and accounts receivable to her work as a healthcare advocate. She learned the job from inside the same system her clients are trying to navigate.

Lindsey worked directly with insurance claims and medical collections for major healthcare systems, including Sentara Health Systems, the Medical College of Virginia, the University of Virginia and affiliates, Chesapeake Regional Medical Center, and Bon Secours Maryview Medical Center. Her daily work included verifying patient eligibility, tracking denials and payment delays, correcting billing and coding errors, and writing detailed, evidence-backed appeals that get rejected claims paid. Most patients meet that same work for the first time, on their own, after a diagnosis or a denial letter.

Before entering healthcare, Lindsey spent nearly five years as a Deputy Clerk of Court, filing and managing civil, criminal, and family court cases. The job sharpened her case management, her precision with procedure, and her persistence in moving a complicated file through a slow system. She brings the same skills to advocacy.

Lindsey is a Certified Professional Collection Specialist, a credential that covers Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) compliance. She is also a Virginia Certified Mediator and a Notary Public, and she has trained, coached, and quality-assured teams of collection specialists. That work gave her a practiced eye for where a claim or a bill has gone wrong and how to fix it.

Lindsey is based in Virginia Beach. She understands both sides of the insurance and billing process: how claims are supposed to work, and where they tend to break down.

What a line-by-line review looks for

A sample hospital statement, marked the way we review a real one. The patient, codes and amounts are made up for illustration.

Statement of services

Sample

Patient: A. SampleDate of service: March 4

Sample itemized hospital statement, with three lines marked for review
Code Description Charge
199285 Emergency visit, level 5 (marked: see note 1) $1,960.00
85025 Blood count (CBC) $86.00
285025 Blood count (CBC) (marked: see note 2) $86.00
71046 Chest X-ray, 2 views $412.00
396374 IV medication, push (marked: see note 3) $318.00
80053 Metabolic panel $148.00
Total charges $3,010.00
  1. 1

    Coded at a higher level?

    Emergency visits are billed at one of five levels. We check that the level on the bill matches the care your visit record describes.

  2. 2

    Billed twice?

    The same blood test appears on two lines for the same day. We ask the billing office to confirm it or take the duplicate off.

  3. 3

    Was this given?

    Every line should match care you actually received. If your records show no IV medication, we ask for the charge to be removed.
